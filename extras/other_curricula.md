# < Other Philosophy Curricula & Learning Paths

While our curated course catalog provides a comprehensive self-guided philosophy education, there are many other excellent curricula and resources available. This page compiles alternative learning paths, structured programs, and complementary resources.

---

## Other Open Curricula Projects

### Open Source Society University (OSSU) - Philosophy
**Link**: [github.com/ossu/philosophy](https://github.com/ossu/philosophy)

A complete self-taught education in Philosophy following a structured curriculum inspired by undergraduate philosophy programs. Features:
- Systematically organized course progression
- Clear prerequisites and learning paths
- Active community of learners
- Regular curriculum updates

**Best For**: Those who want a highly structured, sequential approach with clear prerequisites.

---

### Philosophy Bro's Reading List
**Link**: [philosophybro.com](https://philosophybro.com)

An informal but comprehensive guide to philosophy with:
- Accessible explanations of complex topics
- Curated reading lists for major thinkers
- Modern, relatable writing style
- Good starting point for beginners

**Best For**: Beginners who want accessible, informal introductions before diving into primary texts.

---

### Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (SEP)
**Link**: [plato.stanford.edu](https://plato.stanford.edu/)

The gold standard for philosophical reference:
- Peer-reviewed articles by experts
- Comprehensive coverage of topics
- Regularly updated content
- Extensive bibliographies

**Best For**: In-depth research, understanding specific topics, and academic-quality overviews.

---

### Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy (IEP)
**Link**: [iep.utm.edu](https://www.iep.utm.edu/)

Peer-reviewed encyclopedia covering:
- All major philosophical areas
- Historical and contemporary topics
- More accessible than SEP for beginners
- Excellent for foundational understanding

**Best For**: Complementary reading alongside courses, accessible academic-level overviews.

## Structured Reading Lists

### The Great Books Curriculum
A classical approach focusing on primary texts:

**Ancient Period**:
- Plato's Republic, Symposium, Apology
- Aristotle's Nicomachean Ethics, Metaphysics
- Marcus Aurelius' Meditations
- Epictetus' Enchiridion

**Modern Period**:
- Descartes' Meditations
- Hume's Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding
- Kant's Prolegomena to Any Future Metaphysics
- Nietzsche's Beyond Good and Evil

**Contemporary**:
- Bertrand Russell's Problems of Philosophy
- William James' Pragmatism
- Sartre's Existentialism is a Humanism

**Best For**: Those who prefer intensive engagement with primary sources over lectures.

---

### Mortimer Adler's "How to Read a Book" Philosophy List
A structured approach to philosophical texts with emphasis on:
- Close reading methodology
- Dialectical engagement
- Building a personal philosophical system

**Best For**: Autodidacts focused on developing strong reading and analytical skills.

## Specialized Learning Paths

### Logic & Critical Thinking Track
1. Introduction to Logic (Coursera - Stanford)
2. _An Introduction to Formal Logic_ by Peter Smith
3. _forallx: An Introduction to Formal Logic_ (free textbook)
4. Practice with logic puzzles and proofs

### Ethics & Practical Philosophy Track
1. Justice (Harvard - Michael Sandel)
2. Practical Ethics (Coursera - Peter Singer)
3. _The Elements of Moral Philosophy_ by James Rachels
4. Apply to contemporary case studies

### History of Philosophy Track
1. Ancient Philosophy (Penn or Yale)
2. Modern Philosophy: Descartes to Kant
3. 19th Century Philosophy
4. 20th Century Analytic and Continental

### Philosophy of Mind & Cognitive Science
1. Philosophy and the Science of Human Nature (Yale)
2. Introduction to Philosophy of Mind
3. Consciousness (various MOOCs)
4. Cognitive science readings

## From Self-Study to Academic Philosophy

### Preparing for Graduate Programs
If you're considering academic philosophy:

1. **Develop strong writing skills** - Practice philosophical argumentation
2. **Read widely** - Cover major periods and subdisciplines
3. **Learn languages** - Ancient Greek, Latin, German, French (depending on focus)
4. **Engage with primary sources** - Don't just read about philosophy, read philosophy
5. **Connect with academics** - Email professors, attend conferences
6. **Take the GRE** - Many programs require it

### Useful Resources for Aspiring Academics
- Daily Nous' guide to applying to graduate programs
- The Philosopher's Cocoon (career advice blog)
- Philosophy Talk (various career paths in philosophy)

## < Why Multiple Curricula?

Different approaches work for different learners:
- **Our catalog**: Flexible, modern platform-based courses
- **OSSU**: Highly structured with strict prerequisites
- **Great Books**: Primary text focus, classical education
- **Podcasts**: Audio learning, passive consumption
- **Reading lists**: Self-paced, deep engagement

**Recommendation**: Mix and match! Use courses for structure, podcasts for commutes, primary texts for depth, and encyclopedias for reference.
