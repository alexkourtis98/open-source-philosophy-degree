## Core Philosophy Curriculum

### Foundation Level (Months 1-6)

**Goals**:
- Develop critical thinking and argumentation skills
- Gain historical overview of Western philosophy
- Explore fundamental philosophical questions
- Build vocabulary and reading skills

**Required Courses** (Choose 2-3):

1. **[Philosophy and Critical Thinking](https://www.edx.org/learn/humanities/the-university-of-queensland-philosophy-and-critical-thinking-5)**  University of Queensland
   - **Duration**: 4 weeks
   - **Why**: Essential reasoning skills foundation
   - **Difficulty**: Beginner

2. **[Introduction to Western Philosophy](https://online.hillsdale.edu/courses/promo/introduction-to-western-philosophy)**  Hillsdale College
   - **Duration**: 14 lectures (self-paced)
   - **Why**: Historical survey of major thinkers
   - **Difficulty**: Beginner

3. **[Introduction to Philosophy](https://www.coursera.org/learn/philosophy)**  University of Edinburgh
   - **Duration**: 5 weeks
   - **Why**: Covers multiple philosophical domains
   - **Difficulty**: Intermediate

**Recommended Reading**:
- Simon Blackburn, _Think: A Compelling Introduction to Philosophy_
- Thomas Nagel, _What Does It All Mean?_
- Plato, _The Apology_ and _Crito_

---

### Intermediate Level (Months 7-18)

**Goals**:
- Study core philosophical areas in depth
- Engage with primary sources
- Develop analytical and writing skills
- Understand major philosophical movements

**Core Areas to Cover**:

#### 1. Ethics & Moral Philosophy (2-3 months)

**Required**:
- **[Justice](https://www.edx.org/learn/justice/harvard-university-justice)**  Harvard University (12 weeks)

**Choose 1**:
- **[Practical Ethics](https://www.coursera.org/learn/practical-ethics)**  Princeton (8 weeks)
- **[Introduction to Bioethics](https://www.edx.org/learn/bioethics/georgetown-university-introduction-to-bioethics)**  Georgetown (6 weeks)

**Readings**:
- Aristotle, _Nicomachean Ethics_ (Books I, II, X)
- Kant, _Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals_
- Mill, _Utilitarianism_
- Peter Singer, _Practical Ethics_

---

#### 2. Logic & Critical Thinking (1-2 months)

**Required** (Choose 1):
- **[Introduction to Logic](https://www.coursera.org/learn/logic-introduction)**  Stanford (8 weeks)
- **[Think Again: How to Reason and Argue](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/critical-thinking-reasoning-argumentation)**  Duke (4 courses)

**Readings**:
- Patrick Hurley, _A Concise Introduction to Logic_ (selected chapters)
- Practice logic problems and puzzles

---

#### 3. Metaphysics & Epistemology (2-3 months)

**Required** (Choose 1-2):
- **[Introduction to Philosophy](https://www.coursera.org/learn/philosophy)**  Edinburgh (5 weeks)
- **[Problems of Philosophy](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/24-00-problems-of-philosophy-fall-2019/)**  MIT (self-paced)

**Readings**:
- Descartes, _Meditations on First Philosophy_
- Hume, _An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding_
- Berkeley, _Three Dialogues Between Hylas and Philonous_

---

#### 4. Political Philosophy (2 months)

**Required**:
- **[Introduction to Political Philosophy](https://oyc.yale.edu/political-science/plsc-114)**  Yale (24 lectures)

**Readings**:
- Plato, _Republic_ (Books I, II, V)
- Hobbes, _Leviathan_ (selected chapters)
- Locke, _Second Treatise of Government_
- Rousseau, _The Social Contract_
- Rawls, _Justice as Fairness_ (article)

---

#### 5. History of Philosophy (3-4 months)

**Ancient Philosophy**:
- **[Ancient Philosophy](https://www.coursera.org/learn/ancient-philosophy)**  Penn (9 weeks)

**Modern Philosophy**:
- **[Modern Philosophy: Descartes to Kant](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLqhyMxjGaGxuJq53MUvCLnb5TnMJIkPCO)**  UC Irvine (self-paced)

**Readings**:
- Plato, _Republic_, _Phaedo_, _Symposium_
- Aristotle, _Nicomachean Ethics_, _Physics_ (Book II)
- Descartes, _Meditations_
- Kant, _Prolegomena to Any Future Metaphysics_

---

#### 6. Philosophy of Mind & Science (2 months)

**Required** (Choose 1-2):
- **[Philosophy and the Science of Human Nature](https://oyc.yale.edu/philosophy/phil-181)**  Yale (26 lectures)
- **[Death](https://oyc.yale.edu/philosophy/phil-176)**  Yale (26 lectures)
- **[Philosophy of Science](https://www.coursera.org/learn/philosophy-of-science)**  UC San Diego (4 weeks)

**Readings**:
- Gilbert Ryle, _The Concept of Mind_ (Chapter 1)
- Thomas Nagel, "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?"
- David Chalmers, "Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness"

---

### Advanced Level (Months 19-36+)

**Goals**:
- Specialize in areas of interest
- Read primary sources extensively
- Develop original philosophical positions
- Engage with contemporary debates

**Approach**:
- Take advanced courses in specialized areas
- Read recent journal articles (PhilPapers, Stanford Encyclopedia)
- Participate in online philosophy communities
- Write essays and develop arguments
- Consider formal academic study if interested

**Advanced Course Options**:
- **[Problems of Philosophy](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/24-00-problems-of-philosophy-fall-2019/)**  MIT
- Any graduate-level courses from MIT OCW
- Specialized topics based on interest

## Specialized Tracks

### Track 1: Ethics & Practical Philosophy

**Duration**: 12-18 months

**Sequence**:
1. Philosophy and Critical Thinking (UQ)  4 weeks
2. Justice (Harvard)  12 weeks
3. Practical Ethics (Princeton)  8 weeks
4. Introduction to Bioethics (Georgetown)  6 weeks
5. Introduction to Political Philosophy (Yale)  Self-paced
6. Specialized readings in applied ethics

**Career Applications**: Healthcare, law, policy, nonprofit work, business ethics

---

### Track 2: Logic & Analytical Philosophy

**Duration**: 10-15 months

**Sequence**:
1. Philosophy and Critical Thinking (UQ)  4 weeks
2. Introduction to Logic (Stanford)  8 weeks
3. Think Again (Duke)  4 months
4. Problems of Philosophy (MIT)  Self-paced
5. Advanced logic courses
6. Formal epistemology readings

**Career Applications**: Computer science, mathematics, law, research

---

### Track 3: Philosophy of Mind & Cognitive Science

**Duration**: 12-18 months

**Sequence**:
1. Introduction to Philosophy (Edinburgh)  5 weeks
2. Death (Yale)  Self-paced
3. Philosophy and the Science of Human Nature (Yale)  Self-paced
4. Philosophy of Science (UC San Diego)  4 weeks
5. Specialized readings in consciousness studies
6. Neuroscience/psychology coursework (optional)

**Career Applications**: Psychology, neuroscience, AI/machine learning, cognitive science

---

### Track 4: History of Philosophy

**Duration**: 18-24 months

**Sequence**:
1. Introduction to Western Philosophy (Hillsdale)  Self-paced
2. Ancient Philosophy (Penn)  9 weeks
3. Introduction to Political Philosophy (Yale)  Self-paced
4. Modern Philosophy (UC Irvine)  Self-paced
5. Big Questions in Philosophy (MIT)  Self-paced
6. Extensive primary source reading
7. Contemporary philosophy courses

**Career Applications**: Academic research, teaching, writing, journalism

---

### Track 5: Political & Social Philosophy

**Duration**: 12-18 months

**Sequence**:
1. Justice (Harvard)  12 weeks
2. Introduction to Political Philosophy (Yale)  Self-paced
3. Practical Ethics (Princeton)  8 weeks
4. Ancient Philosophy (Penn)  9 weeks (focus on Plato/Aristotle)
5. Modern political philosophy readings
6. Contemporary social justice topics

**Career Applications**: Public policy, law, activism, journalism, politics

## Year-by-Year Study Plans

### One-Year Intensive Plan

**Time Commitment**: 15-20 hours/week

**Semester 1 (6 months)**:
- Month 1-2: Introduction to Western Philosophy (Hillsdale)
- Month 2-3: Philosophy and Critical Thinking (UQ)
- Month 3-6: Justice (Harvard) + Ancient Philosophy (Penn)

**Semester 2 (6 months)**:
- Month 7-9: Introduction to Logic (Stanford)
- Month 9-11: Introduction to Political Philosophy (Yale)
- Month 11-12: Death (Yale) OR Philosophy of Science (UC San Diego)

**Outcome**: Solid foundation across major philosophical areas

---

### Two-Year Comprehensive Plan

**Time Commitment**: 10-15 hours/week

**Year 1**:
- **Fall**: Introduction to Western Philosophy + Philosophy and Critical Thinking
- **Spring**: Justice + Ancient Philosophy

**Year 2**:
- **Fall**: Introduction to Logic + Introduction to Political Philosophy
- **Spring**: Modern Philosophy + Death OR Philosophy of Mind

**Summer (between years)**: Independent reading and essay writing

**Outcome**: Equivalent to undergraduate minor in philosophy

---

### Four-Year Liberal Arts Plan

**Time Commitment**: 5-10 hours/week

**Year 1: Foundations**
- Fall: Introduction to Western Philosophy
- Spring: Philosophy and Critical Thinking + Justice (part 1)

**Year 2: Core Areas**
- Fall: Justice (part 2) + Ancient Philosophy
- Spring: Introduction to Logic + Introduction to Philosophy (Edinburgh)

**Year 3: Specialization Begins**
- Fall: Introduction to Political Philosophy
- Spring: Modern Philosophy + selected electives

**Year 4: Advanced Study**
- Fall: Problems of Philosophy (MIT) + specialized courses
- Spring: Capstone: extensive reading and writing project

**Outcome**: Equivalent to undergraduate major in philosophy

## Reading Recommendations

### Essential Primary Sources

**Ancient**:
- Plato: _Apology_, _Crito_, _Republic_, _Symposium_
- Aristotle: _Nicomachean Ethics_, _Politics_

**Medieval** (Optional but enriching):
- Augustine: _Confessions_ (selections)
- Aquinas: _Summa Theologica_ (selections)

**Early Modern**:
- Descartes: _Meditations on First Philosophy_
- Hobbes: _Leviathan_ (Part I)
- Locke: _Second Treatise of Government_
- Hume: _Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding_
- Kant: _Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals_

**19th Century**:
- Mill: _Utilitarianism_, _On Liberty_
- Nietzsche: _Beyond Good and Evil_ (selections)

**20th Century**:
- Sartre: _Existentialism is a Humanism_
- Rawls: _A Theory of Justice_ (selections)
- Singer: _Practical Ethics_

### Secondary Sources & Companions

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (online, free)
- Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy (online, free)
- _The Cambridge Companion_ series (various topics)
- _Very Short Introductions_ series (Oxford University Press)

## Assessment & Progress Tracking

### Self-Assessment Strategies

**After Each Course**:
-  Can I explain main concepts to someone else?
-  Can I summarize key arguments?
-  Can I identify objections and responses?
-  Do I understand how this connects to other material?
-  Can I apply these concepts to new situations?

### Portfolio Development

**Maintain a Learning Portfolio**:
1. **Course completion log**: Courses taken, dates, grades (if applicable)
2. **Reading list**: Books and articles read
3. **Notes and summaries**: Organized by topic
4. **Essays and writing**: Original argumentative pieces
5. **Reflections**: How philosophy has influenced your thinking

### Writing Practice

**Progressive Writing Goals**:
- **Foundation**: 500-word summaries of arguments
- **Intermediate**: 1000-1500 word analysis essays
- **Advanced**: 2000-3000 word argumentative essays
- **Capstone**: 5000+ word research paper

**Writing Topics**:
- Summarize and evaluate a philosophical argument
- Compare two philosophers' views on a topic
- Defend a position against objections
- Apply philosophical concepts to contemporary issues

### Certification

**Earning Credentials**:
- Many platforms offer paid certificates (~$50-150)
- Consider earning certificates for significant courses
- Include on resume/CV under "Professional Development"
- Hillsdale offers free certificates

## Customizing Your Curriculum

### Questions to Ask Yourself

1. **What are my learning goals?**
   - Personal enrichment, career development, academic preparation?

2. **How much time can I dedicate?**
   - Adjust pace based on available hours per week

3. **What topics interest me most?**
   - Focus on ethics, logic, metaphysics, political philosophy, etc.?

4. **What's my learning style?**
   - Video lectures, reading-heavy, discussion-based?

5. **Do I want credentials?**
   - Consider platforms offering certificates

### Adaptation Strategies

- **Time-constrained**: Focus on shorter courses, use 1.5x speed for videos
- **Reading-focused**: Prioritize MIT OCW and Yale (extensive reading lists)
- **Discussion-oriented**: Choose Coursera/edX courses with active forums
- **Career-focused**: Select tracks aligned with professional goals

## Final Notes

These guidelines are **suggestions**, not requirements. Philosophy is about:
- **Curiosity**: Follow what interests you
- **Rigor**: Engage deeply with ideas
- **Openness**: Be willing to change your mind
- **Practice**: Think and write regularly

**Create your own path** based on your interests, goals, and circumstances. The joy of philosophy is in the journey of inquiry itself.

**Happy philosophizing!** 🎓
