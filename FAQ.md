# Frequently Asked Questions

## General Questions

### What is the Open Source Philosophy Degree?

The Open Source Philosophy Degree is a curated collection of free, high-quality philosophy courses and resources from top universities and institutions worldwide. Our goal is to make philosophy education accessible to everyone, regardless of financial circumstances or geographic location.

### Is this an accredited degree program?

No, this is not an accredited degree program. It's a self-guided curriculum using free online resources. While the courses are from reputable institutions, completing them through this project does not grant you an official degree. However, many platforms offer certificates of completion (some free, some paid).

### How much does it cost?

Everything in our core curriculum is **completely free**. All courses can be audited at no cost. Some platforms offer optional paid certificates, but these are not required for learning.

### Do I need any prerequisites?

Most beginner-level courses require no prerequisites. Some advanced courses may assume familiarity with basic philosophical concepts. Check individual course descriptions for specific requirements.

## Getting Started

### Where should I begin?

Start with our [Course Catalog](/catalog/) and:

1. **Complete beginners**: Start with courses labeled "Beginner" in the "General & Introductory" category
2. **Some background**: Explore specific areas that interest you (Ethics, Logic, etc.)
3. **Advanced learners**: Jump to intermediate or advanced courses in your areas of interest

Recommended first courses:
- Philosophy and Critical Thinking (University of Queensland)
- Introduction to Western Philosophy (Hillsdale College)
- Death (Yale - surprisingly accessible!)

### How long will it take to complete?

This depends entirely on your pace and goals:

- **Single course**: 4-12 weeks (following recommended pace)
- **Comprehensive survey**: 6-12 months part-time
- **Deep study**: 2-4 years exploring multiple areas thoroughly

There's no deadline. Learn at your own pace.

### Can I pick and choose courses?

Absolutely! This is a flexible, self-guided program. You can:
- Take courses in any order
- Focus on specific philosophical areas
- Skip topics that don't interest you
- Return to courses later

### Do I need to take all the courses?

No. The catalog is a menu of options, not a required curriculum. Choose courses based on your interests and goals.

## Studying & Learning

### How should I approach these courses?

For best results:

1. **Take notes** while watching lectures
2. **Do the readings** even if not required
3. **Complete assignments** when available
4. **Engage in forums** to discuss ideas
5. **Reflect** on how concepts apply to your life
6. **Revisit** difficult concepts multiple times

### Should I do the readings?

Yes! Philosophy is best learned through:
- Primary sources (original philosophical texts)
- Active engagement with arguments
- Critical thinking and analysis

Don't just watch videosread the assigned texts.

### What if I don't understand something?

Philosophy can be challenging. Try these strategies:

1. **Re-watch/re-read** the material
2. **Take breaks** and let ideas permeate
3. **Use supplementary resources** (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy)
4. **Discuss** with others in course forums
5. **Move on and return later** with fresh perspective

### Can I discuss courses with others?

Most courses have discussion forums. Additionally:
- Join philosophy discussion groups on Reddit (r/philosophy, r/askphilosophy)
- Participate in online philosophy communities
- Form study groups with other learners
- Contribute to our project discussions

### How do I stay motivated?

- **Set a schedule**: Dedicate specific times for study
- **Track progress**: Keep a learning journal
- **Join communities**: Connect with other learners
- **Apply concepts**: Relate philosophy to your life
- **Celebrate milestones**: Acknowledge course completions
- **Stay curious**: Follow your interests

Minimal requirements:

Yes! Most platforms have:

## Philosophy-Specific Questions

### I'm new to philosophy. Where do I start?

Begin with:
1. **Introduction to Western Philosophy** (Hillsdale) - Historical overview
2. **Philosophy and Critical Thinking** (UQ) - Reasoning skills
3. **Justice** (Harvard) - Engaging, accessible ethics

Then explore areas that intrigue you!

### What are the main branches of philosophy?

Our catalog is organized by major areas:

- **Ethics**: Study of morality, right and wrong
- **Logic**: Principles of valid reasoning
- **Metaphysics**: Nature of reality and existence
- **Epistemology**: Theory of knowledge
- **Political Philosophy**: Government, justice, rights
- **Philosophy of Mind**: Consciousness, mental states
- **Philosophy of Science**: Nature of scientific inquiry

### Do I need to be good at math for philosophy?

Most philosophy doesn't require math. However:
- **Formal logic** uses symbolic notation (learnable without math background)
- **Philosophy of mathematics** obviously involves math
- **Some epistemology** touches on probability theory

Critical thinking and reading comprehension are more important than mathematical ability.

### How is philosophy useful in everyday life?

Philosophy develops:
- **Critical thinking**: Analyze arguments and claims
- **Ethical reasoning**: Navigate moral dilemmas
- **Clarity of thought**: Articulate ideas precisely
- **Intellectual humility**: Recognize limitations of knowledge
- **Problem-solving**: Approach complex issues systematically
- **Communication**: Express ideas persuasively

These skills apply to any career or life situation.
